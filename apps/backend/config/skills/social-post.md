---
name: Writing a social post from the data
when_to_use: The user asks for a social post, caption, carousel or Instagram slides — "buatkan konten", "caption untuk IG", "post about last month's numbers".
---

A post is one finding, told to people who did not ask for it. A report can
hold everything; a post that tries to loses the reader at the first slide.

1. **Query first, write second.** Run the queries for the period and the cut
   the user named, read the figures, and only then write the caption. Every
   number in the caption appears on a slide, and every number on a slide came
   from a query in this turn. **A price is the strictest case of this rule:**
   a promotion is a public, quotable claim about what something costs, so read
   both the old and the new price out of the data and never round, estimate or
   infer a discount you did not compute from two real figures.
2. **Pick the one finding.** The largest change, the record, the thing the
   audience would repeat to a friend. Everything else is a slide in a
   different post. If the data holds no story — flat month, small counts —
   say so and offer the honest angle rather than manufacturing a headline.
3. **Name the period and the source on the cover or in the caption**, in
   plain words: "Agustus 2026, semua cabang". A post has no report header, so
   the reader cannot tell last month from this month unless it is written.
4. **Decide first whether this is one image or a carousel.** An announcement
   is one image: a promotion, a launch, a price, one figure worth repeating.
   A product with a price is a single `promo` section — badge, product name,
   the photograph by name, the old price and the new one. Anything else is a
   single `hero` section — kicker, headline, one supporting line. Either way,
   stop there. Do not add slides to reach a number; a padded carousel reads as a
   template, and the swipe that finds nothing is worse than the post that
   never asked for one.
5. **A carousel is five to seven slides, one claim each.** Cover with the
   hook; the headline figure as a kpi row; one chart for the breakdown; a
   callout for the so-what; a closing slide with the ask. A table only when
   the reader will want to find their own row in it. More than ten is refused;
   more than seven is usually a report.
6. **Ask for a size only when the user names a shape.** The default is the
   4:5 feed post. "Story" or "status" means `story`; "kotak" or "square"
   means `square`. A story is read full screen with the app's own controls
   over the top and bottom, so it holds less than its height suggests.
7. **The first line of the caption stands alone.** Feeds show roughly the
   first 125 characters before "more", so the hook goes there and does not
   depend on what follows. Then two to four short lines, then the ask — the
   store link, the promo, the question to the audience — if the user has one.
8. **Percentages travel with their base.** "+40%" on its own reads as
   marketing; "+40%, dari 120 ke 168 pesanan" reads as a fact. For any
   comparison, follow the period-over-period procedure; on social its rule
   about a prior period of zero matters twice, because a post is quoted.
9. **Hashtags: three to eight, specific before generic.** The brand, the
   product, the city, the campaign; one broad tag at most. Put them in the
   spec's `hashtags` list without the `#`, not inside the caption text.
10. **Match the user's language and the platform's register.** Indonesian
   question, Indonesian post; social is informal unless the user's own
   caption examples or a workspace procedure say otherwise. Do not translate
   product names or figures' formatting.
11. **Do not post it.** The slides and the caption are delivered for a person
   to publish; the tool says the carousel is being made, and that is what to
   tell the user.
